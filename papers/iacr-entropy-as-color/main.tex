\begin{abstract}
We study a deterministic presentation and audit layer that assigns elements of the finite field $\mathbb{F}_3$ and corresponding color tiles to observations from heterogeneous entropy sources. The labels make policy balance and execution-order invariance inspectable, but they neither create entropy nor establish cryptographic security. We define the label algebra, prove its elementary conservation identities, and connect those identities to a Julia implementation and Lean statements. We also state the source-model, extractor, and reduction obligations that would be required to turn this audit layer into a cryptographic construction. Our principal contribution is therefore a falsifiable separation between measured source evidence, deterministic representations, and security claims.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{Entropy Sources \and GF(3) \and Audit Labels \and Reproducibility \and Randomness Extraction}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Cryptographic systems depend on validated entropy sources and random-bit
generator constructions. Existing standards carefully separate noise-source
models, health tests, entropy estimates, conditioning, and deterministic
generation. Our concern is narrower: experimental software can erase those
distinctions by naming arbitrary recorded words ``entropy'' and by presenting
deterministic labels as security evidence.

We study a deliberately modest labeling discipline: recorded observations may
carry elements of $\mathbb F_3=\{-1,0,+1\}$ and corresponding display colors.
The QCD vocabulary is mnemonic only; it supplies no physical or cryptographic
claim.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{GF(3) Audit Algebra}: We define ternary labels with operation $\oplus_3$ and separate their algebraic invariants from entropy estimates (\Cref{sec:algebra}).
    
    \item \textbf{Representation Boundary}: We map labels to color tiles while making explicit that colors and hashes represent observations rather than identify sources or grant authority (\Cref{sec:color}).
    
    \item \textbf{Security Non-implication}: We show why label conservation alone implies neither extraction nor composable security and give the missing proof obligations (\Cref{sec:security}).
    
    \item \textbf{Executable Reference}: We connect deterministic label composition to the Gay.jl implementation and its tests (\Cref{sec:implementation}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}
%------------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Notation}

Let $\mathbb{F}_3 = \text{GF}(3) = \{-1, 0, +1\}$ denote the finite field of three elements with addition modulo 3. We use the term \emph{trit} for elements of $\mathbb{F}_3$, with semantic labels:
\begin{align}
    \text{MINUS} &\triangleq -1 & \text{(validator/constrainer)} \\
    \text{ERGODIC} &\triangleq 0 & \text{(coordinator/synthesizer)} \\
    \text{PLUS} &\triangleq +1 & \text{(generator/executor)}
\end{align}

\subsection{Entropy Sources}

Following NIST SP 800-90B~\cite{nist80090b}, a cryptographic entropy source
requires a noise-source model, digitization, health testing, and a justified
min-entropy estimate. The implementation studied here does not establish those
properties. We therefore distinguish a source from its recorded observation.

\begin{definition}[Source observation]
A source observation is a tuple
\[
o=(\rho,t,x,\ell,w),
\]
where $\rho$ is a typed source referent, $t$ is a logical timestamp,
$x\in\{0,1\}^{64}$ is an opaque sample word, $\ell\in\mathbb F_3$ is a policy
label, and $w\in[0,1]$ is an application-supplied vote weight. Neither $x$ nor
$w$ is, by type alone, a min-entropy estimate.
\end{definition}

Gay.jl's compatibility type \texttt{ColoredTick} stores $(t,\ell,x,w)$ and a
symbolic source label. Its historical field \texttt{entropy} stores $x$; the
name must not be read as evidence that $H_\infty(X)>0$.

\subsection{QCD analogy boundary}

Quantum chromodynamics motivates some terminology, but supplies no theorem
about software observations. Our colors are presentation tiles; they are not
physical charges, source identities, entropy estimates, or security labels.

\section{GF(3) Entropy Algebra}
\label{sec:algebra}
%------------------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{definition}[Trit]
A \emph{trit} is an element $t \in \mathbb{F}_3 = \{-1, 0, +1\}$ with addition defined as:
\[
a \oplus_3 b \triangleq ((a + b + 1) \mod 3) - 1
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Policy label]
Given a real-valued application measurement $m(o)$ and configured thresholds,
the observation label $\tau(o)\in\mathbb F_3$ is:
\[
\tau(o) = \begin{cases}
    +1 & \text{if } m(o) > \theta_{\text{high}} \\
    0 & \text{if } \theta_{\text{low}} \le m(o) \le \theta_{\text{high}} \\
    -1 & \text{if } m(o) < \theta_{\text{low}}
\end{cases}
\]
where $\theta_{\text{low}}\le\theta_{\text{high}}$ are application policy.
Unless $m$ is separately validated as an entropy estimator, $\tau$ makes no
statement about min-entropy.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Conditional label balance]
\label{thm:conservation}
For labels $a,b,c\in\mathbb{F}_3$, if $c=-(a+b)$, then:
\[
a \oplus_3 b \oplus_3 c = 0.
\]
This algebraic statement does not imply that three measured sources are independent, unpredictable, or secure.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By substitution, $a+b+c=a+b-(a+b)=0$ in the additive group of $\mathbb{F}_3$. See \Cref{app:proofs} for the exact Lean cross-reference.
\end{proof}

\section{Color Charge Formalism}
\label{sec:color}
%------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Building on the trit algebra, we introduce a color presentation inspired by QCD.

\begin{definition}[Color tile]
A recorded observation may be rendered with a \emph{color tile}
$\chi(o)\in\mathcal C$, where:
\[
\mathcal{C} = \{\text{RED}, \text{GREEN}, \text{BLUE}, \text{ANTI\_RED}, \text{ANTI\_GREEN}, \text{ANTI\_BLUE}, \text{COLORLESS}\}
\]
\end{definition}

The color-to-trit mapping $\phi: \mathcal{C} \to \mathbb{F}_3$ is:
\begin{align}
    \phi(\text{RED}) = \phi(\text{ANTI\_RED}) &= +1 \\
    \phi(\text{GREEN}) = \phi(\text{ANTI\_GREEN}) &= 0 \\
    \phi(\text{BLUE}) = \phi(\text{ANTI\_BLUE}) &= -1 \\
    \phi(\text{COLORLESS}) &= 0
\end{align}

\begin{definition}[Display balance]
A set of recorded observations $\{o_1, \ldots, o_n\}$ is
\emph{display-balanced} if:
\[
\bigoplus_{i=1}^n \phi(\chi(o_i)) = 0 \pmod{3}.
\]
This predicate is a property of representations under $\phi$, not of the
identity, independence, or unpredictability of the represented sources.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Visual Representation.}
Color charges map to the HSL color wheel:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{PLUS (+1)}: Warm hues (0°--60°, 300°--360°)
    \item \textbf{ERGODIC (0)}: Neutral hues (60°--180°)
    \item \textbf{MINUS (-1)}: Cool hues (180°--300°)
\end{itemize}

\section{Security Analysis}
\label{sec:security}
%------------------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{proposition}[Label conservation is not extraction]
\label{thm:security}
GF(3) label balance alone implies no lower bound on min-entropy and no
computational indistinguishability guarantee.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take three deterministic constant sources and assign them labels $+1,0,-1$.
Their labels balance, while their joint output has min-entropy zero. Thus label
balance cannot imply entropy amplification, pseudorandomness, or universal
composability.
\end{proof}

A cryptographic construction would additionally need a security parameter, a
joint or conditional source model, an explicit seeded or multi-source
extractor, output length and statistical-error parameters, and either a game
or an ideal functionality with an adversary, environment, and simulator. The
present implementation is an audit layer and is not such a construction.

\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:implementation}
%------------------------------------------------------------------------------

We provide a reference implementation in Julia as part of the Gay.jl package.
The following algorithm describes \texttt{composite\_from\_readings}; it does
not reject unbalanced labels and does not output cryptographic randomness.

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Deterministic observation summary}
\label{alg:composition}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Nonempty observations $o_i=(\rho_i,t_i,x_i,\ell_i,w_i)$
\Ensure Summary $(t^*,x^*,\ell^*,w^*)$
\State $x^* \gets x_1\oplus\cdots\oplus x_n$
\State $v \gets \sum_i w_i\ell_i$
\State $\ell^* \gets +1$ if $v>0.33$, $-1$ if $v<-0.33$, and $0$ otherwise
\State $w^* \gets \operatorname{mean}_i(w_i)$
\If{all $\ell_i$ are equal}
    \State $w^* \gets \min(1,1.2w^*)$
\Else
    \State $w^* \gets 0.8w^*$
\EndIf
\State $t^* \gets t_j$ for an index $j\in\operatorname{argmax}_i t_i$
\State \Return $(t^*,x^*,\ell^*,w^*)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The empty-input implementation returns a distinguished all-zero summary.
Separately, \texttt{entropy\_mix} applies SplitMix64 to a seed XORed with one
sample word and an encoding of its label. Both operations are deterministic;
neither estimates entropy, checks source independence, performs health tests,
or instantiates a NIST conditioning component.

\subsection{Illustrative sensor policy}

As a non-empirical example, a policy could label three sensor classes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{LiDAR timing jitter}: PLUS (+1)
    \item \textbf{Camera CCD noise}: ERGODIC (0)
    \item \textbf{Radar Doppler variance}: MINUS (-1)
\end{itemize}

The labels satisfy $+1 + 0 + (-1) = 0$. This says only that the policy is
balanced; source quality, dependence, and fitness for TPM seeding require
separate measurement and analysis.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
%------------------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Entropy-source standards.} NIST SP 800-90B~\cite{nist80090b}
specifies entropy-source design, health testing, min-entropy estimation, and
conditioning. NIST SP 800-90C~\cite{nist80090c} composes 90B entropy sources
with 90A DRBG mechanisms into defined RBG constructions. RFC 4086
~\cite{rfc4086} likewise warns that statistical appearance is not
unpredictability, correlated XOR inputs can fail, and mixing cannot increase
the unpredictability present in its inputs. Gay.jl's observation summary is
not a substitute for any of these mechanisms.

\paragraph{Extraction.} Fuzzy extractors formalize extraction of nearly
uniform key material from noisy, nonuniform inputs under explicit entropy and
distance assumptions~\cite{dodis2004extractors}. Our labels are not an
extractor seed, secure sketch, or entropy bound.

\paragraph{Visual cryptography.} Naor and Shamir's visual secret
sharing~\cite{naor1995visual} encodes secrets into visual shares. Our colors
instead display audit metadata and carry no secret-sharing property.

\paragraph{Provenance.} W3C PROV distinguishes entities, activities, agents,
derivations, responsibility, and alternate or specialized descriptions of the
same thing~\cite{w3cprov}. A source observation can be represented as a PROV
entity and the deterministic summary as an activity. Our typed referent and
representation boundary is therefore not a new general provenance model; it
is a domain-specific restriction on how entropy-related metadata may be
interpreted.

\paragraph{Authenticated artifact assurance.} in-toto records materials,
products, commands, and functionaries in signed link metadata checked against
a signed supply-chain layout~\cite{intoto2019}. SLSA provenance similarly
binds build outputs by digest to their producing build~\cite{slsa11}. Our EDN
ledgers are unsigned local conformance records: they provide neither
functionary authentication nor tamper-resistant provenance. Deployments that
need those properties should carry these records inside an authenticated
provenance system rather than treating a color, symbol, or hash as authority.

\paragraph{Novelty boundary.} We do not claim a new RNG, extractor, entropy
estimator, robust combiner, provenance model, supply-chain assurance system,
or security definition. The limited contribution is a GF(3)-valued,
domain-specific view over source referents, recorded sample words, policy
labels, weights, color tiles, and deterministic summaries, together with
executable negative gates that reject promotion of representation invariants
into entropy, identity, authority, or cryptographic claims. Its value must be
judged as an audit specialization, not as a replacement for PROV, in-toto,
SLSA, or the NIST random-bit-generation standards.

\section{Artifact Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}
%------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The evaluation asks only questions supported by the audit-layer contribution:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Does the claim ledger reject an asserted claim whose state is
    \texttt{unverified} or \texttt{contradicted}?
    \item Do the referent and interaction validators reject representations
    that claim identity or authority?
    \item Does the implementation produce a deterministic summary matching
    \Cref{alg:composition} for a fixed observation vector?
\end{enumerate}

On Julia 1.12.6 and Apple arm64 Darwin 25.5.0, the committed commands produced:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\hline
Check & Positive result & Negative witnesses \\
\hline
Claim ledger & 10 claims resolved & asserted unsupported states rejected \\
Referent boundary & 3 referents, 5 laws & 5 malformed cases rejected \\
Interaction boundary & 4 interactions, 8 laws & 9 malformed cases rejected \\
Summary fixture & sample word $10\oplus12=6$ & not a security test \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

For the two-observation fixture with labels $+1,-1$ and weights $0.9,0.8$,
the summary is deterministic, has label $0$, and has adjusted weight $0.68$.
The commands and expected outputs are recorded in \texttt{artifact.edn}.

\paragraph{Limitations.} These checks validate conformance and rejection
behavior, not novelty, usability, source entropy, statistical randomness,
side-channel resistance, or cryptographic security. There is no performance
claim and therefore no timing benchmark. An independent artifact evaluator
has not yet reproduced the results.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%------------------------------------------------------------------------------

We have specified a GF(3)-valued audit and presentation layer for entropy-source observations. Its conservation identities are elementary and executable, while its colors remain representations rather than security evidence. The counterexample in \Cref{thm:security} prevents a balanced label policy from being mistaken for entropy extraction. Future work must begin with a precise source model and extractor construction before making cryptographic security claims.

\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{thm:conservation}]
The additive inverse axiom in $\mathbb{F}_3$ gives
$a+b-(a+b)=0$. The repository theorem
\texttt{balanced\_iff} in \texttt{lean4/gf3\_elegant.lean} states the
corresponding equivalence. The nearby theorem \texttt{char\_three} proves the
distinct characteristic-three identity $t+t+t=0$. Neither theorem mentions
entropy sources, independence, or cryptographic security.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{thm:security}]
Let each source output the all-zero string with probability one. Assign the
three observations labels $+1,0,-1$. The label sum is zero, but every function
of the joint deterministic sample is deterministic and has min-entropy zero.
This proves the non-implication.

\paragraph{Formal-verification scope.} The checked-in Lean sources include
machine-checkable finite-field identities such as \texttt{balanced\_iff} and
\texttt{char\_three}. They do not formalize an entropy-source distribution,
extractor, random-oracle game, or UC ideal functionality. We therefore make no
machine-verification claim for a cryptographic theorem.
\end{proof}
